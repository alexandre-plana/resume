# Natural French patterns

Read this reference when drafting, rewriting, summarizing, or polishing prose with the `writing-natural-technical-french` skill.

## Prefer a positive shape

Natural professional prose usually answers four questions in a useful order:

1. What is the subject or situation?
2. What happened or what must be understood?
3. What supports or explains it?
4. What consequence matters to the reader?

Not every passage needs all four. Use only the parts that carry information.

## Patterns to correct

### Importance without evidence

Words such as *stratégique*, *innovant*, *majeur*, *significatif*, *robuste* or *à forte valeur ajoutée* require evidence or a precise comparison. Remove them when they merely intensify the sentence.

Describe the property instead: the system is not “robust” because it has tests; state what is tested, recovered, replicated, monitored, or measured.

### Empty professional formulas

Watch for:

- « dans le cadre de » when a direct subject works;
- « j'ai eu l'opportunité de » instead of the performed action;
- « contribuer activement », « apporter de la valeur » or « répondre aux enjeux » without a named contribution;
- « cette expérience enrichissante m'a permis de » when the useful information is the work itself;
- « mise en œuvre d'une solution permettant de » when a concrete verb can name the change.

Do not delete a phrase solely because it appears on this list. Replace it when it hides the actor, action, evidence, or consequence.

### Nominal chains and catalogues

Long sequences such as « conception, structuration, industrialisation et amélioration » conceal who did what. Prefer a verb with an object and enough context to understand the responsibility.

Do not turn every paragraph into short subject–verb–object sentences. Combine closely related facts when the relation is clear; separate them when the sentence asks the reader to retain several different ideas.

### Mechanical rhythm

Repeated openings, identical paragraph lengths, systematic three-part lists, and forced contrasts make prose feel generated. Vary structure only when the reasoning changes. Necessary transitions may be short and need not carry a new fact if they make the relationship between facts clear.

### Technical vocabulary

Keep established domain terms when they are more precise than a paraphrase. Explain a term when the expected reader may not know it or when misunderstanding would change the conclusion. Avoid stacking technologies where one named mechanism is enough.

When a CV or portfolio deliberately uses `#terms` as inline technical chips, preserve the terms that identify a relevant technology, standard, or protocol throughout the prose fields requested by the user. Attach each chip to the contribution it explains instead of collecting chips in a stack at the end. The surrounding sentence must remain understandable without the visual treatment. Verify that the presentation layer renders chips in every targeted field; do not assume support from one field applies to the others.

## Before and after

Source facts:

> Développement d'un module de gestion des alertes. Aucun résultat chiffré ni retour utilisateur n'est disponible.

Avoid:

> Dans le cadre de cette mission stratégique, j'ai eu l'opportunité de contribuer activement à la mise en œuvre d'une solution innovante permettant d'optimiser efficacement la gestion des alertes et d'apporter une réelle valeur ajoutée aux parties prenantes.

Prefer:

> J'ai développé un module de gestion des alertes destiné aux opérateurs. Faute de mesure disponible, le texte ne prétend pas qu'il a réduit le temps de traitement ou amélioré la satisfaction des utilisateurs.

In a CV, omit the second sentence unless the absence of evidence itself matters. The concise version is then:

> Développement d'un module de gestion des alertes destiné aux opérateurs.

## CV-specific naturalness

- Use the first person in a short profile when it sounds like the candidate; omit it in compact achievement bullets.
- Prefer role and contribution over self-assessment. Let work demonstrate curiosity, rigor, seniority, or leadership.
- Connect selected technical chips to the product context or action they support; do not replace the contribution with a technology list.
- Replace task inventories with a few contributions that distinguish the candidate.
- A result may be qualitative when exact: reused by several teams, shipped on three platforms, operated offline, or designed for a regulated environment. Do not convert scope into impact.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Making the prose “more senior” by strengthening claims | Show scope, decisions, constraints, or responsibility that the sources support |
| Removing every transition in pursuit of density | Keep transitions that express a real relationship |
| Replacing repeated technical terms with loose synonyms | Keep the stable term |
| Removing technical chips to make the prose look cleaner | Retain verified, discriminating chips and anchor them to the relevant contribution |
| Adding chips only to the summary card | Apply the convention to every prose field in scope and verify rendering support for each field |
| Treating every CV field as having the same technical density | Respect field-specific intent; a positioning paragraph may remain chip-free while experiences carry the technical detail |
| Hiding uncertainty to sound decisive | State the known value, source, date, or uncertainty |
| Adding numbers for credibility | Use only documented numbers |
| Rewriting sound passages during every pass | Revise only failed passages |

## Reader pass

Read the final text once as the intended reader. Check that the subject is identifiable, the main point arrives early, the technical depth is useful, sentence patterns are not mechanically repeated, and no ending merely restates the opening.
